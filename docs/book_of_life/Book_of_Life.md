# Title: Book of Life

## Introduction

In order to address the metacrisis facing humanity, we must first describe and understand the concepts necessary to describe that metacrisis.  These concepts must not only be able to describe coherently our current situation and give us valuable insights into the nature of the problem.  In order to be truly useful, they must also help us understand other systems in novel ways, and then through such analysis, we may begin to understand the general pattern of the problem, and how existing solutions in biology are pointing us toward a path forward.

The basic idea that I would like to orient around is the idea of identity.  In the modern political climate, identity has become the central and polarizing concept, and it is clear that we are not understanding it well enough to address the issues that it is causing.  We get varied messages from various communities, your identity is who you think you are.  Society stratifies into classes, or races, or  genders, or religions, or nationalities, and this is your identity, your "group" identity.  And finally we come to a new pseudo-scientific idea of intersectionality as a way to describe the interactions between these various group identities in the individual to contribute to their oppression or privilege.

But what is an identity, do we even have a definition that is sufficiently generalizable that we believe it's use is sound and valid?

Into this void, I started to think about the various ways in which we classify people, and what those classifications offer?  If I look at myself, and think of the labels applied to me, I find that I am a father, a son, a husband, a brother, a co-worker, a boss, an employee, a Christian, a programmer, a customer, a friend, a neighbor, a citizen, a human being, oh, and I'm male..., and I'm white.  

When I started to think about these labels, what was the point of them?  What does it say to label me a "father"?  And to whom?  Is it merely to myself?  Or is it to my children also? To my wife?  To my friend?  To my boss?  To my community?

And from this most simple of inquiries into the nature of identity you instantly realize that identity works in two ways simultaneously both to myself and to others.  Which of these should take precedence?  Should the use towards myself be primary?  Or should the use towards others be primary?  Great question?  My only question there would be what would be be in isolation? Would I still have an identity?

Now, I don't think you can actually consider a human being as a thing alone in the world in isolation.  But we may come back to this later in order to more fully explore the use value of identity, and then consider through other applications of identity the meaningful nature of identity in isolation.

Is there a more broad category of which identity is an instance.  In particular I was noticing the inward facing and outward facing of the terms or "group" identities that we use to describe ourselves and others. And I thought of this as a boundary.  At very least in discussing a human being as distinct from the rest of the world, the skin forms a boundary that distinguishes the physical body from everything "not me" in the world.

And if we look at boundaries in the most general sense, we can see that they appear naturally in nature, and are created by human beings in the physical world, and are an element of our personal relationships.  A boundary is a division between two things; it represents the limit of a thing, that delineates between two spaces.

But, one might ask, just because reality manifests a boundary, doesn't make it the "right" boundary.  Doesn't necessarily make it more true than any other way of conceiving of the world does it?

I mean, do I see a cell membrane as a definitive boundary?  Or the brain as having a boundary?  Can I differentiate between the nervous system and the circulatory system at a boundary?  How about an atom?  Do I make that boundary at it's outer shell of electrons?  Or do I consider the nucleus as the boundary?  Where do I place these boundaries?  By what process do I make a conjecture about where a boundary is?  By what process do I decide that such a boundary is in fact meaningful, true, or real?

And this brings me to the most general of capacities, circumscription.  The act of delineating a boundary that separates the world into 2 distinct spaces.  Note that there are an infinite number of ways to circumscribe volumes in space, in order to consider the phenomena that occur within that space as opposed to everything that happens outside that space.

Now notice, that without describing the "correctness" or "truth" of a specific boundary that we draw, we will find that SOME circumscriptions will have an interesting feature of being harmonic or resonant in a way that other circumscriptions are NOT.  I might offer a set of examples. 

Here are a list of interestingly "resonant" circumscriptions:
1. Solar System
2. Human Body
3. Cell
4. Atom
5. Proton
6. City

Not that circumscription, does not in fact isolate the specific phenomena from the rest of reality.  In each case there is an exchange of information between the circumscribed phenomena and the rest of reality.  It is not an isolation into a self-contained or closed system.  In each case we can even see how we could expand or contract the boundary slightly because at the edges there are various components which are fringe elements, necessary perhaps, but distinct in some way from the circumscribed phenomena.  But the resonance holds, and is distinct.
